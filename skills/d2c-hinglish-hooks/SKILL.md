# d2c-hinglish-hooks

Indian buyers scroll in Hinglish. Copy translated from English reads like a
government notice; copy written in natural Hinglish reads like a friend. This
skill writes the second kind, and checks it so it does not get the ad rejected
or the brand in trouble.

## Step 1: the brief

Ask in one message:

1. Product and price, and what makes it different (be specific: material,
   finish, weight, warranty, delivery time).
2. Who buys it and for what moment (self, gift, wedding, office, festival).
3. The offer, if any, and when it ends.
4. Proof they can actually show: reviews count, rating, a real customer quote,
   a certification. Only use proof they confirm.
5. Language: Hinglish (default for Indian audiences), English or Hindi. Roman
   script unless they ask for Devanagari.
6. Format: static, carousel, Reel or UGC video.

## Step 2: hooks

Write 10 hooks, each under 10 words, across different formulas. Label each one.

| Formula | Example (fashion jewellery) |
|---|---|
| Price anchor | "₹799 ke jhumke, ₹7,999 wala look" |
| Problem | "Har shaadi mein wahi purane earrings?" |
| Myth-bust | "Oxidised jewellery kaali nahi padti, agar ye karo" |
| Stop doing | "Gold-plated set lene se pehle ye check karo" |
| POV | "POV: Sangeet mein sab tumhare jhumke pooch rahe hain" |
| Comparison | "Mall mein ₹3,000, yahan ₹999. Fark dekho" (only with a real price comparison) |
| Social proof | "2,000 orders ke baad bhi ye hamara no. 1 kyun hai" (only if true) |
| Curiosity | "Is set ka asli cost sunke shock ho jaoge" |
| Occasion urgency | "Karwa Chauth tak delivery chahiye? Aaj order karo" |
| Gifting | "Bhai, Rakhi pe phir se paise mat dena. Ye do" |

Then score your own hooks out of 10 on three things: specific (numbers, names,
objects), relevant (speaks to this buyer's moment) and honest (no claim the
brand cannot back). Recommend the top 3 and say why.

## Step 3: primary text and headlines

- 3 primary texts. The first 125 characters must work alone, because that is
  roughly what shows before "See more". Hook, benefit, offer, then CTA.
- 5 headlines, 40 characters or less.
- One clear CTA per ad: "Shop now", "Order karo", "COD available".

## Step 4: the Reel pack (for video)

```
Hook (0–2s): spoken line + on-screen text (max 6 words) + the first shot
Beat 2 (2–6s): show the product in use, close-up
Beat 3 (6–15s): the one reason to believe (finish, price, comfort, reviews)
Beat 4 (15–22s): the occasion or styling moment
CTA (last 3s): offer + "link pe click karo" / "COD available"
Shot list: numbered, phone-friendly, no special equipment
Caption: first line works alone; up to 5 hashtags
```

Keep Reels between 15 and 30 seconds unless the user asks otherwise.

## Step 5: compliance check (run on every line)

- **Claims:** no "pure gold", "100% silver", "anti-tarnish", "hypoallergenic",
  "lowest price in India" or "best quality" unless the brand can prove it.
  Plated is plated; say "gold-plated".
- **Meta personal attributes:** do not assert or imply things about the viewer's
  body, health, finances or identity ("Kya aap moti hain?" will be rejected).
- **Fake urgency:** no countdowns or "last 2 pieces" unless true.
- **ASCI:** influencer and UGC ads need a clear disclosure label. Under ASCI's
  guidelines on AI in advertising, AI-generated people, cloned voices or
  made-up scenes need a label; routine AI edits and AI-written copy do not.
- **Customer quotes:** only real ones, used with permission.

Flag anything that fails and give a compliant rewrite next to it.

## Rules

- Write like a person, not a brand manual. Short lines. No "Elevate your
  style", "Unleash", "Discover the magic".
- Mix Hindi and English the way buyers actually talk; do not translate
  word for word.
- Never invent reviews, ratings, order counts or celebrity names.
